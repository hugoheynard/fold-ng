The full-screen mobile navigation, in two levels. A grid of large tiles; a tile that contains tiles opens a second level instead of going somewhere. It owns the whole overlay contract: scrim, Escape / close-button dismissal, focus-trap, body scroll-lock — and the descent itself.

  1. The tile you touch becomes the anchor. It does not disappear: it climbs into the level-2 header at pastille size. It is the only element that survives the transition, so it is the only landmark the eye needs.
  2. Its sisters leave in a wave that starts under your finger — 26 ms per step of distance, not per position in the list. They rise as they blur, upward, where the finger is not going.
  3. Level 2 arrives as a list from below, on the tone of the secondary rail, one seam away from the primary. That is the desktop's own relation: descending into a section is moving from rail 1 to rail 2, not from furniture to content. A list and not a grid — "Price-list templates" does not fit in a square, and a list is read with the thumb.
  4. A row with no icon falls back to a status dot, in the same gutter. A silent row must not shift its neighbour.
  5. The way back is the exact inverse, not another animation. Same curve, same cascade, opposite direction — which is what makes the depth understood without thinking about it.

The launcher finds the depth by content query, exactly as fold-multiselect finds its fold-optgroup. Two levels and no more — at a third this stops being a launcher and becomes a tree. The entry count in the header is derived, never passed: a count declared beside the list it counts starts lying the first time someone adds a tile without touching it.
  • Focus follows the level — down to the sheet's first entry, back to the tile it came from. It moves only once the new level is actually painted; taken a frame early it failed silently and the trap fell back to the close button, so the caret landed on "dismiss" every time.
  • Escape is contextual — at level 2 it climbs, at level 1 it closes. Two gestures, one key, in that order.
  • The level is announced — a polite live region says "PIM, 7 entries".
  • The swipe is a bonus, not the path. It doubles the back button; it never replaces it.

The launcher stays dark: glass does not brighten the surfaces, it brightens the light that crosses them. The ground drops a step, a veil of three haloes appears behind, and every plane trades opacity for translucency. Without the veil the blur has nothing to blur and you get darker panels, nothing more.
